# Negotiation prep (example)

Copy this to `~/.live-call-assistant/negotiate/prep.md` and fill it in for each negotiation, or run `/call-negotiate prep` and Claude will help you write it. `/call-negotiate` reads it at the start of each call. It combines the Black Swan Group's one-page prep sheet (see the [playbook](playbook.md#prepare-the-one-page-plan)) with the private framework from the [field guides](resources/). Everything is optional: keep what helps.

**Make the skill yours:** put your own playbooks, scripts and style notes in the same folder, as any other `.md` files. The skill reads them too, and follows them over the shared playbook where they differ.

Everything in that folder stays on your computer, outside the repo, and is only sent to Claude when you use the skill.

## The negotiation

- What: <for example, the offer call for a role after the final round; a contract renewal with a client; a price with a supplier>
- Who: <who you'll talk to, and who decides behind the table>
- When: <date and time>

## 1. Goal and private framework

- Ideal (the goal you plan around): <specific terms you'd be genuinely pleased with>
- What it's based on: <market data, the band, other offers, scope>
- If they ask for my number first: <deflect, or anchor to the role with a credible range; which, and the words I'll use>
- My "sign today" number (private): <what I'd sign for on the spot>
- Timing: <when I expect other offers, and when I'll decide>
- My planned pushes: <for example, 1. ask them to improve it without a number; 2. "closer to X"; 3. "if you can do Z, I'll sign">
- If base won't move, my asks in order: <for example, signing bonus, scheduled increase, extra leave>
- Acceptable deal: <the whole package you'd actually accept>
- Walk-away (private; never said out loud): <the point below which you'd say no>
- Hard boundaries (not for trade): <for example, trust in the manager, workload, role content, wellbeing, location>
- My alternative if there's no deal: <for example, continuing the search, another client>

## 2. Summary

<The situation in two or three sentences the other side would answer "that's right" to.>

## 3. Labels and accusation audit

- Accusation audit: <"You're probably going to think I'm asking for a lot…">
- Labels: <"It seems like the budget was set before the scope grew." "It sounds like you're under pressure to close this soon.">

## 4. Calibrated questions

- To qualify the deal: <"What would I actually spend most of my time on in the first six months?" "What's funded and approved, and what's still a hope?" "Who makes the final call when we disagree?">
- To solve problems: <"How can we bridge the gap?" "What would need to change for this to be approved?">
- About behind the table: <"How does approval work from here?">

## 5. Trades

- They could give: <for example, a scheduled increase, a signing bonus, a review with criteria, title, start date, leave, remote days, training, scope, timeline>
- I could give: <for example, an earlier start, a longer commitment, a smaller first phase>
- What I'd ask in return for each concession: <…>

- <anything else: what they've said before, numbers from earlier rounds, how they like to negotiate, what to watch for>
