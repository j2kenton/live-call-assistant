# 15 rules for negotiating a job offer (Deepak Malhotra)

Source: Deepak Malhotra, *15 Rules for Negotiating a Job Offer*, Harvard Business Review, April 2014 ([hbr.org/2014/04/15-rules-for-negotiating-a-job-offer](https://hbr.org/2014/04/15-rules-for-negotiating-a-job-offer)). The 15 rules are confirmed by more than one source; the detail under each comes from a single reprint. Summarised in our own words.

Its theme: the relationship and the person across the table matter as much as the numbers.

## The rules

1. **Don't underestimate likability.** People only fight for you if they like you. "I'm really excited about this team. I just want to sort out a couple of things so I can say yes with full enthusiasm."
2. **Help them see why you deserve it.** Never let a number stand alone: tell the story behind it. "Given that I'd own X from day one, and what similar roles pay, I'm hoping for Y."
3. **Make it clear they can get you.** Nobody fights for someone they think will say no. If you mention other options, say what would make you set them aside. "If we can get to Y, I'm ready to sign this week."
4. **Understand the person across the table.** A hiring manager and HR care about different things. "What matters most to you in getting this role filled?"
5. **Understand their constraints.** Bands and internal pay equity may be real limits. "What's fixed here, and where is there some flexibility?"
6. **Prepare for tough questions** ("Do you have other offers?", "Would you accept tomorrow?", "Are we your top choice?"). Don't lie, and don't give away your position. "I'm talking with a couple of other companies. This one is a strong fit, which is why I want to get the details right."
7. **Answer the intent, not just the question.** "Would you accept tomorrow?" usually means "how keen are you?". "Is the real question how interested I am? Because I'm very interested."
8. **Consider the whole deal:** role, flexibility, growth, start date, not just pay. "If base is fixed, can we look at the signing bonus, equity or start date?"
9. **Negotiate several issues at once, not one at a time,** so they can't settle the easy ones and call it meeting you halfway. "There are three things I'd like to discuss, in order of importance…"
10. **Don't negotiate just to negotiate.** Haggling over small points uses up goodwill: drop the minor ones.
11. **Think about timing.** Slow one process down or speed another up so offers arrive close together, without pulling back so far that you lose an employer.
12. **Avoid, ignore or downplay ultimatums.** Don't issue them. If you get one, sidestep it so they can back down without losing face: "I understand that's a constraint. Could we look at the other parts of the package?"
13. **Remember they're not out to get you.** Delays usually come from internal constraints, not hostility. "Is there anything I can send that would help things along on your side?"
14. **Stay at the table.** A no today can become a yes later: "Could we revisit this at the six-month review?"
15. **Keep perspective.** Getting the right job matters more than winning the negotiation.

## Alongside the other sources

- **Rule 9 (everything at once) vs Doody (base first, then benefits one at a time):** offer as alternatives. Raising everything together protects against being worn down item by item; going one at a time keeps each ask simple and focused.
- **Rule 3 echoes Qureshi's "be winnable"** and interviewing.io's "if you can do Z, I'll sign today".
