# Salary negotiation for engineers (Patrick McKenzie)

Source: Patrick McKenzie, *Salary Negotiation: Make More Money, Be More Valued*, Kalzumeus Software, 23 January 2012 ([kalzumeus.com/2012/01/23/salary-negotiation](https://www.kalzumeus.com/2012/01/23/salary-negotiation/)). Summarised in our own words. Written in 2012 with US examples; the principles have aged well.

A classic for engineers: negotiating takes minutes and pays off for years, and it's a joint problem to solve, not a fight.

## Moves

- **Talk money only after a "yes, if".** Someone with authority should have agreed in principle to hire you, subject to terms. If pay comes up before that: "I'd like us to find out first whether we're a good fit. I'm sure we can sort out the numbers after that."
- **Don't give the first number.** Each time they push, restate that fit comes first and the numbers are flexible. "We need a number to move forward" is usually less firm than it sounds.
- **React to a low offer with "interesting".** Positive, and it commits you to nothing: "That's an interesting number. Can we talk about getting it closer to where it needs to be?"
- **Listen, take notes, and use their words.** When you argue your value, reuse their own description of the problem they need solved.
- **Trade new information for concessions.** Bring in a result you haven't mentioned yet, and put a value on it: "At my last company I [improved X by Y]. What would that be worth here? Next to that, the gap we're discussing is small." Useful when they say the budget is fixed.
- **Set salary aside and trade across levers.** If salary is stuck, move to something else and trade between them ("I could accept that base with [an extra week of leave]"). If HR controls salary, ask about what the manager controls: leave, hours, projects, travel, training: "I understand salary is capped. What can you do on [training]?"
- **Get it in writing.** After each verbal agreement, send a short recap and ask them to confirm.
- **Don't sound needy.** Keep enthusiasm for the role clearly conditional on terms that work.
- **Exploding offers:** ask for the time you need: "Would tomorrow give you enough time to run the numbers?"

## Prep

- **Research the company itself:** talk to current and former employees about what it values and which levers move easily. Salary sites are only a rough baseline.

## Where it differs: offer as an alternative

- **If a number is truly unavoidable,** McKenzie's last resort is a total-package figure rather than base salary. That can reveal what you earn now, which interviewing.io and Doody advise never doing. Offer it only when refusing any number would end the process, and frame it as total compensation for this role, not your current pay.
