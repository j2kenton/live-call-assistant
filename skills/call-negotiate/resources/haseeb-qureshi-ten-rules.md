# Ten rules for negotiating a job offer (Haseeb Qureshi)

Sources: Haseeb Qureshi, *Ten Rules for Negotiating a Job Offer* (11 July 2016, [haseebq.com/my-ten-rules-for-negotiating-a-job-offer](https://haseebq.com/my-ten-rules-for-negotiating-a-job-offer/)) and *How Not to Bomb Your Offer Negotiation* (2 September 2016, [haseebq.com/how-not-to-bomb-your-offer-negotiation](https://haseebq.com/how-not-to-bomb-your-offer-negotiation/)). Summarised in our own words, with the honesty adjustments noted. Silicon Valley, 2016: the figures are dated; the rules aren't.

## The rules

1. **Get everything in writing.** After each call, note the details and confirm them by email: "Just confirming I have the details right."
2. **Keep the door open.** Asked "so what do you think?", don't commit and don't judge the numbers: "I'm really excited about this, and I'm confident we can find something that works. I'd like some time before getting into specifics." Asking for time is the most valuable thing to ask for on a first offer.
3. **Information is power.** Share nothing they can use against you. If your current pay must come up, describe the whole package (bonus, unvested stock, an upcoming promotion) and that you're looking for a step up.
4. **Stay positive,** even when the offer disappoints. Name something you genuinely like about the role or the team.
5. **Involve the people who really decide with you.** "I'll go over this with [my partner / my family / a mentor] and come back to you." Or, more simply: "I don't make decisions like this on the spot. I'll come back to you by [day]."
6. **Have alternatives.** Real ones: other processes, staying in your current job (speak well of it), further study. Tell companies you're actively talking to when you have an offer, and ask them to move faster.
7. **Give a reason for every ask.** Even an ordinary reason makes an ask feel more legitimate: "I'm hoping to buy a home this year. What can we do on base?"
8. **Care about more than money.** Ask about the team, the first project, a mentor, the title, training. It has to be genuine.
9. **Understand what's easy for them to give.** Usually salary is hardest, a signing bonus easier, equity easiest. Annualise equity grants, and check vesting terms.
10. **Be winnable.** Give them a clear path to closing you, and don't go silent. If nothing would make you sign, decline rather than negotiate.

## Moves for pressure

- **"If I get you X, will you commit?":** "I can't commit yet, but I'd really appreciate you pushing for it." Promise only what you'd do.
- **Asked not to shop the offer:** politely decline to promise that.
- **Their deadline:** set your own, say it early, and hold to it. A deadline that doesn't work for you: "That deadline doesn't work for me. Could we look at [date]?"
- **The close, only at the very end:** "If you can do X, I'll sign."

## Where it differs: offer as alternatives

- **Naming other companies:** Qureshi tells companies about competing offers, by name if notable, to speed them up. interviewing.io advises saying little. Offer naming as the option when speed matters more than secrecy.
- **Phone or email:** Qureshi likes the phone for rapport (email if you're nervous); interviewing.io and Doody prefer email for the counter.
- **If forced to give a number:** Qureshi anchors to an objective benchmark, softer than Doody's single number 10–20% above the offer.
